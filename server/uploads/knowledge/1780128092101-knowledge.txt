# Jewellery Industry: Overview, Operations, Pricing, Manufacturing, Inventory Management and Customer Sales

## Introduction

The jewellery industry is one of the oldest and most valuable industries in the world. Jewellery serves multiple purposes including personal adornment, cultural significance, investment, gifting, wealth preservation, and fashion. In countries such as India, jewellery holds a particularly important place due to traditions, weddings, festivals, and investment preferences. Gold, silver, diamonds, and precious gemstones form the foundation of the jewellery business.

Modern jewellery businesses range from small family-owned retail stores to large multinational brands operating across multiple countries. The industry combines craftsmanship, precious materials, manufacturing expertise, inventory management, pricing calculations, and customer service to create and sell jewellery products.

The jewellery industry consists of multiple stages including procurement of raw materials, manufacturing, quality assessment, inventory management, pricing calculation, retail sales, invoicing, and after-sales services.

---

## Types of Jewellery

Jewellery products are generally classified into several categories.

### Gold Jewellery

Gold jewellery is the most popular category in many countries. Gold jewellery may include:

* Rings
* Necklaces
* Bangles
* Bracelets
* Earrings
* Pendants
* Chains
* Wedding sets

Gold jewellery is available in different purity levels such as:

* 24K Gold
* 22K Gold
* 18K Gold
* 14K Gold

Purity significantly affects pricing and valuation.

### Silver Jewellery

Silver jewellery is commonly used for:

* Daily wear
* Fashion jewellery
* Traditional ornaments
* Religious items

Silver jewellery generally has lower material costs compared to gold jewellery.

### Diamond Jewellery

Diamond jewellery combines precious metals with natural or laboratory-grown diamonds.

Examples include:

* Diamond rings
* Diamond earrings
* Diamond necklaces
* Bridal jewellery

Diamond valuation depends on the 4Cs:

* Cut
* Color
* Clarity
* Carat Weight

### Gemstone Jewellery

Gemstone jewellery incorporates various precious and semi-precious stones including:

* Ruby
* Emerald
* Sapphire
* Topaz
* Opal
* Amethyst
* Garnet

Each gemstone has unique characteristics and market pricing.

## Jewellery Manufacturing Process

The manufacturing process transforms raw materials into finished jewellery products.

### Design Stage

The process begins with jewellery design.

Designers create:

* Hand sketches
* CAD models
* 3D prototypes

Modern jewellery manufacturers frequently use computer-aided design software.

### Casting Process

After design approval:

* Wax models are prepared.
* Molds are created.
* Precious metal casting is performed.

This process creates the basic structure of the jewellery item.

### Stone Setting

Gemstones and diamonds are carefully mounted into the jewellery structure.

Stone setting techniques include:

* Prong setting
* Bezel setting
* Channel setting
* Pavé setting

### Polishing and Finishing

Jewellery items undergo:

* Surface finishing
* Polishing
* Cleaning
* Quality inspection

The objective is to achieve a premium appearance and customer-ready product.

## Gold Purity and Tunch

Gold purity is one of the most important aspects of jewellery pricing.

The term "Tunch" is widely used in the jewellery industry to represent purity levels.

Examples:

* 999 Tunch
* 995 Tunch
* 916 Tunch
* 750 Tunch

A higher tunch value indicates higher gold purity.

Gold purity directly affects:

* Metal value
* Resale value
* Customer trust
* Hallmark certification

---

## Hallmarking

Hallmarking is a certification process used to verify the purity of precious metals.

Hallmark certification helps:

* Protect customers
* Improve trust
* Ensure legal compliance

A hallmark typically contains:

* Purity mark
* Assaying center mark
* Jeweller identification mark
* Hallmarking year code

## Jewellery Weight Concepts

Accurate weight measurement is critical for jewellery pricing.

### Gross Weight

Gross weight refers to the total weight of the jewellery item.

It includes:

* Gold
* Silver
* Diamonds
* Gemstones
* Other components

### Net Weight

Net weight generally refers to the weight of the precious metal after excluding removable components.

### Pure Weight

Pure weight represents the actual weight of pure precious metal present in the jewellery.

Pure weight calculation often incorporates purity percentages.

## Components of Jewellery Pricing

Jewellery pricing involves multiple components.

### Metal Value

Metal value is calculated using:

Metal Weight × Current Market Rate

For example:

Gold Weight × Gold Rate

### Diamond Value

Diamond pricing depends on:

* Weight
* Quality
* Shape
* Certification

### Gemstone Value

Gemstone pricing depends on:

* Type
* Weight
* Quality
* Origin

### Wastage Charges

Jewellery manufacturing generates material loss.

To compensate for this loss, jewellers often apply wastage charges.

Wastage percentages vary depending on:

* Product type
* Manufacturing complexity
* Design intricacy

### Labour Charges

Labour charges cover:

* Design work
* Manufacturing effort
* Finishing work
* Stone setting

Labour may be:

* Fixed amount
* Percentage based
* Weight based

### Other Charges

Additional charges may include:

* Certification fees
* Packaging charges
* Logistics costs
* Custom design charges

## GST in Jewellery Industry

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is applicable to jewellery transactions.

Businesses must:

* Maintain GST registration
* Generate GST invoices
* Report taxable transactions

GST verification systems can automatically validate:

* GST Number
* Business Name
* Registration Status

This improves compliance and reduces errors.
